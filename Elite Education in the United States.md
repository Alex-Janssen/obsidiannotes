# Elite Education in the United States

## Education as Class Hierarchy
American colleges have become harder and harder to get into. At the same time, they are easier and easier to succeed within. As elite jobs (software, banking) and elite grad programs narrow their selection criteria within these top universities, we see a world in which our elite is being selected from a very narrow, privileged band of students who have the resources to compete in the college admissions rate race. 
There is an idea of cookie-cutter molding of aristocrats needed to get into top colleges. You need to serve others, noblesse oblige, be good at a million things but have a specialty. This deprives students of the ability to actually chase curiosity, fail, and become original thinkers. Instead, admissions success hinges on a narrowly legible idea of what education means. Education, in this mold, means success. Never getting a B, never getting below a 1450 on the [[SAT]].
Thus, we have a caste of privileged leaders who don't much interact with the rest of the world. They interact with a geographically, racially diverse set of upper-middle to upper class people. They get to sit in the same room as incredible researchers they don't get to much talk with. When they are in the university, they are handed easy successes, and, without the ability to reflect, tend to follow paths of least resistance. It pats students on the back for being selected into this group, giving them a sense of superiority which carries forward into how they lead the rest of society who they never tend to meet.
College is a time to forge your own way of thinking through the world - current American education abdicates itself of that responsibility. The funding model being through students means that [[Grade Inflation]] is rampant, the prestige of professorship being publication means they have no incentive to teach. Instead, it becomes a "meritocratic" class marker of success, of acceptability into finance, law, medicine, which will, in turn, make your children wealthy.
Harvard doesn't educate - it distinguishes.

Arguments written as a response to reading of [[Excellent Sheep]]. 

The primary output of education nowadays is pure identity capital as [[The Defining Decade]] discusses - impressiveness for impressiveness's sake.
