## Rule
A contract requires a bargained for return promise or performance to be enforceable, else it lacks consideration.

## Holding
A written promise of housing in exchange for moving and selling possessions is insufficient to constitute consideration and is not enforceable.

## Posture
Appeal from Circuit Court of Talladega, who held the defendant in breach of contract. 

## Facts
The brother of the late husband of the plaintiff wrote to the plaintiff:
"I would advise you to ... sell the land and quit the country, .. If you will come down and see me, I will let you have aplace to raise your family." The plaintiff abandoned her possession and moved with the defendant, who gave her housing and land for two years, after which she got moved to an uncomfortable shack, then was evicted. The plaintiff was on public land prior, held a lease, and would have attempted to secure the land.

## Issue
Whether consideration was formed in the above contract.

## Disposition
Reversed.

## Reasoning
There was no consideration (just a condition) and this was a gratuity as coming down to see the defendant was not considered a sufficient return performance to constitute a contract - thus despite the plaintiff acting in accordance to the letter, there is no expectation of return performance. The action of coming down to see him was not bargained for - he did not actively seek this performance.

## Learning Outcome
Learning consideration

## Objection
The plaintiff was sufficiently inconvenienced by the promise in the letter as to support enforcement of the return promise.
