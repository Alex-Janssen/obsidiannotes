## Rule
Changes in contractual terms for one side require consideration; a promise to continue performance previously bargained for is not consideration.

## Holding
The sailors' refusal to work without higher wages broke prior obligation and thus continuance of the prior obligation was not consideration.

## Posture
District court held that the contract was enforceable.

## Facts
Appellees given $50 to $60 monthly to maintain ship rented for season to be returned, plus 2 cents per salmon caught. Appellees threatened to cease work unless paid an additional $100 not in contract. Due to lack of replacements, the superintendent acquiesced. After completing their duties, they were informed that they would be paid the old rate, and sued.

## Issue
Is the superintendant's promise to pay the demanded increase in wage is enforceable?

## Disposition
Reversed and remanded, appellees to pay costs.

## Reasoning
The new contract for higher wage contained no performance not previously bargained for, and thus there was no consideration, and the promise was therefore not enforceable. The prior obligation was broken, and thus the appellees were liable for damages.

## Learning Outcome
Strictness of consideration doctrine, see [[Levine v. Blumenthal]]
