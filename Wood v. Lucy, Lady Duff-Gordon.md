## Rule
Where a contract grants an exclusive right or agency, courts imply a promise to use reasonable efforts to exploit it, and thus it is enforceable as consideration is established.

## Holding
A promise wherein a promisor gives a percent of revenues for placed endorsements is enforceable due to the fact that the profit incentive generates an impulse for reasonable efforts (to find contracts and opportunities) which generate profit for the promisor. 

## Posture
Appelate division ruled in favor of defendant, appealed by plaintiff.

## Facts
The defendant is a trendsetter who lends her name to products which gain benefit for being associated with her. The plaintiff was given the right with her approval to issue endorsements. She was to have half of the revenues generated by these contracts and the plaintiff the other. This exclusive right was held for a year, auto-renewing, cancelable with 90 days' notice. Plaintiff alleges defendant issued endorsements without his knowledge, keeping 100% of profits.

## Issue
Whether a promise (exclusive licensture) without explicit return promise or performance but with an implied promise is enforceable.

## Disposition
Reversed with costs.

## Reasoning
Defendant argued that this promise did not bind the plaintiff to anything, Cardozo holds there is a fair implied promise that he will use effort to place her endorsement on products. Explicit promises are not needed at time of writing as long as there is an "instinct with an obligation." She gave him exclusive rights to her endorsements or designs for a year autorenewing and there was an implied return promise to work towards creating these placements to the benefit of her (getting 50% of profits).

## Learning Outcome
Consideration does not require spelled-out performance or promise as long as one is reasonably implied.
