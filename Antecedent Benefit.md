# Antecedent Benefit
Promises made in relation to an antecedent benefit are binding to the extent necessary to prevent injustice (R2K 86) to the extent that the benefit was not a gift (not unjust) or if the value is undue with relation to the antecedent benefit. Antecedent benefit as past [[Consideration]] is typically unbinding, although a promise to pay after necessary services, such as emergency care, is considered binding due to an acknowledgment of the necessity of care (a promise of payment changes the nature from favor to exchange).
"A saves B's life in an emergency and is totally and permanently disabled in so doing. One month later B promises to pay A $15 every two weeks for the rest of A's life, and B makes the payments for 8 years until he dies. The promise is binding."
