## Rule
In the case that a promisee puts themself at a state of disadvantage due to a promise, and that this action is reasonable and foreseen by the promisor, this constitutes equitable estoppel and the promise is enforceable if lack of enforcement would result in injustice. 

## Holding
The promisory note induced the plaintiff to quit her job and put herself at disadvantage, which was known to her late grandfather in giving the promisory note, and thus the note should be paid out of the estate.

## Posture
The district court of Lancaster ruled that the plaintiff recover damages - the defendant appealed.

## Facts
A promisory note held "I promise to pay to Katie Scothorn {plaintiff} on demand, $2,000, to be at 6 per cent per annum." The note induced her to quit her occupation and to rely on the 2k and interest to survive. The grandfather of the plaintiff called the plaintiff promising that she would not have to work while presenting the promisory note - and she immediately quit. The grandfather paid a year of interest on the note but not the balance prior to death.

## Issue
Whether the promisory note is legally binding after the plaintiff's grandfather's death in his estate.

## Disposition
Affirmed

## Reasoning
The plaintiff did not receive this benefit in exchange to pursuing or refraining from the pursuit of any conduct. Her right to the money was not contingent on the quitting of the job - her reliance was not obligated by the terms of the note. For churches/schools, there is precedent that gifts induce expenditure, which is sufficient consideration. Simpson Centenary College v. Tuttle, 71 Ia. 596 held that want of consideration does not apply when there was prior engagement or expenditure based on a promise that would lead to injury if not fulfilled. An expectation of performance by the payee without a contract is not enforceable, but a right of action does arise when the payee puts themselves in a position of disadvantage. The grandfather foresaw in giving the gift her granddaughter quitting her job, and thus the payee's disadvantage was caused by faith in the promisory note and this was knowable to the grandfather.
