# Consideration

## Bargain
The formation of a contract requires a bargain in which the exchange is mutually agreed to and a consideration. Consideration requires a return promise or performance, which must be sought by the promisor for his promise and is given by the promisee in exchange for their promise. Performance may be a non-promisory act, forbearance, or creation/destruction/alteration of a legal relation. A performance or return promise may be given to the promisor or some other person by the promisee or some other person.
A bargain requires a quid-pro-quo, both the promisor and promisee must seek the other's performance for consideration to apply. See [[Kirksey v. Kirksey]]

Past consideration is impossble as bargaining (in exchange for) is inherently future looking. "A gives emergency care to B's adult son while the son is sick and without funds far from home. B subsequently promises to reimburse A for his expenses. The promise is not binding under this Section" due to the fact that there was nothing B bargained for - benefit was antecedant to promise.
