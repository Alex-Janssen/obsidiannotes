## Rule
Harsh treatment for those in rackateering and money laundering is acceptable to deter individuals toward crime.

## Holding
All of the defendants' assets should be seized to deter any idea of financial incentive for this crime.

## Posture
Blarek and Pellecch were arrested and charged with racketeering, conspiracy to money-launder instruments, one for interstate travel, and pled not guilty.

## Facts
Two decorators became involved with a drug cartel's operations. 
 Their successful decorating business became tailored to serve one criminal client, and their profits came from their client's drug wealth - this served as money laundering; they converted ill-gotten cash into assets removable from the US. Telephone conversations showed defendant aware of his status, and bookkeeping was highly secret. They lived a flashy lifestyle on these profits.

## Issue
How to sentence the individuals for their aid of drug cartels' operations.

## Disposition
Sentenced so that all assets were seized.

## Reasoning
Four factors for punishment: incapacitation of the criminal, rehabilitation of the offender, deterrence of the defendant and of others, and just deserts for the crime committed. Retributivists believe in just deserts, utilitarians a balancing act of cost-benefit for punishments. [[Immanuel Kant]] believed that punishment must follow a crime because a crime was committed; [[Jeremy Bentham]] believed punishment must be for the greater good of society. For him, deterrence and reformation were the purposes of punishment - punishment was mischief. In this case, the defendants had no ties to the criminal world remaining. They knew how to live a lawful life, rehabilitation had no place. Deterring these individuals had no place, they regretted it, but deterring society at large did here - the case became notorious and in a utiilitarian sense could dissuade others. Retribution fits, as white collar crimes harm society.

## Learning Outcome
Explicit deterrence mentioned - " The sentences are designed to penalize the defendants for their criminal behavior and to deter other business and professional people from assisting drug traffickers."
