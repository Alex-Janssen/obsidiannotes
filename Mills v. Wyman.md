## Rule
A promise requires consideration bargained for in exchange, not moral obligation.

## Holding
Promises based on moral obligation based on [[Antecedent Benefit]] with no future performance or return promise bargained for are unenfoceable, except when originating from preexisting equtable obligations.

## Posture
Appeal from Court of Common Pleas who did not find the promise enforceable.

## Facts
The defendant's 25 year old son was saved by the plaintiff from illness and poverty, and  the defendant (father) promised to pay the plaintiff's expenses. There was no consideration beyond famial ties to the benefited party. 

## Issue
Whether the letter promising payment for fees to save the plaintiff after the saving had occurred is a binding promise.

## Disposition
Affirmed

## Reasoning
In no way was the son requesting this care, nor was the son under the care of the defendant. The plaintiff acted as a good Samaritan. Moral obligation is seen as insufficient consideration due to lack of universality in case law. Moral obligations founded upon antecedent consideration have a sound legal basis, and should be good to support an implied promise, but prior law has left this to the moral conscience rather than legally enforceable. These obligations are binding on the conscience but not law - despite being a violation of moral duty, the lack of payment or promise means the contract lacked consideration.

## Learning Outcome
Limits of moral obligation.

Idea of son being 25 and outside of family is quite relevant - is that a marker of the times?
