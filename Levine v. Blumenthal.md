## Rule
Changes in contractual terms for one side require consideration, a promise to continue performance previously bargained for is not consideration.

## Holding
A promise to decrease rent prices post-fact without any bargained for performance or exchange promise is not enforceable due to lack of consideration.

## Posture
District court sided with plaintiff, appealed by defendant.

## Facts
Plaintiff leased defendant store location for woman's clothing in Paterson, with 2.1k first year, 2.4k second year and optional renewal. Defendant asked to use first year's rent for second year due to lack of business, and there was non-payment for last month, and there was a dispute as to whether the differential was also owed. 

## Issue
Whether the subsequent decrease in rent is enforceable despite lack of consideration.

## Disposition
Affirmed with costs.

## Reasoning
The district court found that the alteration of rent did not come with consideration and was unenforceable. Consideration is held to be fundamental to the idea of contract law, but payment of part of a debt combined with promise to not go bankrupt is not sufficient as it is not clear that the creditor bargained for this. Consideration is held to be a "tangible new obligation or a duty not imposed by the lease" which this does not hold. Economic adversity does not suffice either.

## Learning Outcome
Strictness of consideration doctrine.
