# The Defining Decade

## Introduction
(Don't like the Noam Chomsky quote at the beginning, he's referring to the linguistic [[Critical Period Hypothesis]] which is only true in terms of acquiring an accent from what I have read on more recent literature but hrm.)
Book was inspired to push back against the idea that the thirties are the new twenties.
"I told Kate that while most therapists would agree with Socrates that “the unexamined life is not worth living,” a lesser-known quote by American psychologist Sheldon Kopp might be more important here: “The unlived life is not worth examining."" Amazing
Not worth looking toward the past, toward trauma without having future momentum.
Lack of intention leads to waking up one day not knowing where you are (Kate story).

Past 20s for [[Baby Boomers]] - one income of married household, people stayed in one career for life. Divorce and [[Birth Control]] were just becoming mainstream, median home price was $17,000.
2000s, less than half of 30 year olds are married. Opening of period between graduating college and family formation, new developmental period. Cultural obsession with these years at the same time that it's in vogue to not have direction during them, celebrity culture is geared towards acting 20.
American optimism in the 20s, deferring payment for lack of direction until it catches up.
1/3 of 20s move in a given year (wow).
Cost of freedom - uncertainty.
"We may hear that thirty is the new twenty, but—recession or not—when it comes to work and love and the brain and the body, forty is definitely not the new thirty."
The adult pressures, getting promotion, family formation, graduate education, get piled into a tiny amount of time in the thirties - can't stack everything without a productive 20s.
20s should be a time of adulthood, a time of defining a future. 20s aren't downtime, they are a developmental sweet spot.
Delayed adulthood does give a time of development back to the 20s - use it to become the adult you want to rather than waste it. Easiest time in life to change your trajectory.

## Work

Initial story, premed abandoned for art, didn't stick with photography, and so didn't have stability of med school, practice as photographer and so chose freedom as escape through 27.
Navel gazing at 20s is unproductive.
"Supposed to be having your crisis" - lol.
Erik Erikson - identity crisis, believe that identity formation should not be rushed. Had a life that wasn't clear, but earned Identity Capital through education, networking etc.
Jobs, test scores, how we present ourselves, network are all identity capital. Resources tied to our being societally. How we "purchase" jobs, relationships. < I disagree with this take, it increases probability, but I think purely focusing on achievement > result is reductionist. Probably good prescription, not actually true.
Disengaged confusion is negative, leads to irrelevance.
Freedom is often wrapper over that confusion - explore + commit.
Sometimes lives are built not on resume highlights but on other pieces of identity capital (her outward bound experience, in my life languages+travel have opened doors).
Undermployment is not always a means to an end - bad jobs can do active harm.
20s are easiest time to move fast.

### Weak Ties
Urban tribe - group of closest friends are mythologized.
People that we hardly know, however, are much more likely to strongly impact your life.
Friendship is mostly about similarity, which makes them amazing to lean on but weak for anything other than support.
Weak ties are much more likely to have different views, different strengths, and thus add to your life professionally/romantically/development-wise. (Disagree with this, more just that there's a lot more people with weak ties and you are likely to have weak ties with people with more success, and people are willing to help people they know leads to weak ties having more influence. Highly skeptical that similarity is the root cause.)
Networking is critical for the above reasons.
People favor you more if the do favors for you ([[Benjamin Franklin]] effect).
People tend to want to help unless the ask is overwhelming - use that to your advantage. (huge belief of mine)
If you ask for someone's time, have good questions. Make yourself interesting and relevant.

### The Unthought Known
Being confused about choices is a form of passivity. Don't pretend like there isn't some life that you want to live.
The longer you wait, the fewer options you have. Life is not limitless.
More choices of jam led to more interest in samples and less purchases.
What would you do if you didn't win the lottery? (My thought experiment about the smallest life you'd be happy with).
An unthought known is something you know about yourself that you don't factor into your self-conception. Wanting something and not knowing if you can get it is scary, easier to pretend you have no idea of what you want.
Not making choices isn't safe, it defers.
Shoulds are bad (in this guy's case, prestige of law or interestingness of Arabic versus boringness of design).

### My Life Should Look Better on Facebook
Facebook is an archaic term for Instagram.
"Within months, I was living a lonely and depressing life in [[San Francisco]]." SF will do it to you!
Graduate school will give you A's, life won't.
Facebook is used as surveillance of friends, not keeping up (yup).
People compare themselves to the most successful, best traveled, most romantically successful friends.
People want to grow toward their potential, but that is too vague to be meaningful.
Some dream small, not trying to set themselves up for success, some dream incredibly big and unrealistically.
Ideals don't say anything true about ourselves in the world.
Our people, place, and job define our lives.
Settling isn't to be afraid of - if you know your place and have capital, go.

### The Customized Life
"A person’s identity is not to be found in behavior… but in the capacity
to keep a particular narrative going."
Dread of being someone who has existed before.
Distinctiveness is a part of identity. That being said, it is much easier to say who you aren't than who you are.
Claiming something early is good, it's a base to expand from, but it temporarily limits the idea of endless identity. Do it anyway (specialization is the quickest way to mastery).
Our idea of [[Progress]] has moved to customization.
An authentic life is like a custom bike - your own, but made of standard parts that work together.
In your 20s, potential is more important than proof - become an excellent storyteller. A story should be complex but cohesive.
In any application, your past and present should clearly flow into the future.

## Love
### An Upmarket Conversation
Marriage is the largest determinant of happiness, but only downmarket resources, like reality shows, discuss this predicament. 
Today's 20s are more single than ever.
Most Americans still marry even though serial monogamy and hookup culture are on the rise.
Desire for stable partnership is common and somehow taboo. Desire for career is more socially acceptable.
Revising a career is much easier than revising a bad marriage.
Divorce still couples you to your ex - you can leave a bad job off a resume.
Divorce leads to negative outcomes, despite what current culture says about destigmatizing it.
After age 25, marrying later does not lead to lower divorce rates.
More low commitment relationships can train negative habits for marriage.
At 30, there's pressure to find a partner then, don't waste time not being picky in your 20s.
The best time to work on a marriage is before you're married.

### Picking Your Family
YAVIS—young, attractive, verbal, intelligent, and successful.
YAVIS clients either have good lives with hiccups that are addressed through therapy or bad lives that seem perfect on the outside - difficult!
Hiding your troubles behind success can be "falling up."
A good relationship can be a fill-in support.
People with bad families both need good partners more than anyone and are unequipped with the experience to find them.
Your partner's family partially becomes your own family.
Your partner's family, you two together, and kids are the family that will carry you through the rest of your life.

### The Cohabitation Effect
Living together isn't necessarily a good test for marriage.
Two partners often have different agendas for cohabitation.
Living together doesn't automatically make a partner fix themselves, take themselves more seriously.
Cohabitation can slide into uncertainty about marriage.
Marriage shouldn't be the easy alternative to breaking up - it never is.
Cohabitation has lock-in, easy to set-up, hard to get out of.
Cohabitation partially melds two lives so that breaking up is too much of a hassle, finding a better partner even if you know they aren't the one is difficult.
Switching costs are high - people marry under pressure to not be left out and then pay the costs of divorce.
Cohabitation makes it so the continuation of the relationship isn't necessarily founded in love, which leads to the effect that cohabiting couples are more likely to be divorced in future marriage.

### On Dating Down
Hookup culture often leads to dating down and shame.
Often the hardest thing to cure is coping methods - harder to cure alcoholism/self-harm/negative self-talk than direct problems often.
People often want to be wanted.
People who want you feel like an oasis, if there's no one, a desert.
Adolescence is often more defining than early childhood in terms of self-identity.
Our first stabs of repeated storytelling about ourselves often become the foundation of who we see ourselves as.
Personalities change slower than self-narrative.
Dating down can mean dating where you see yourself as an old, inaccurate version of yourself.

### Being in Like
Easy to get a relationship with commitment before deeply liking the other person.
People are good at avoiding deal breakers, but bad at seeking match-making qualities.
Similar personalities tend to lead to better relationships.
Moving personality to the forefront, i.e. in Online dating, probably leads to better results.
Sometimes people are just poor matches for your personality, but we read them as actual flaws.
Being neurotic is far more toxic to relationships than being mismatched in other ways.
Be picky in things that matter in 20 years, love, shared values and goals. Small differences are a part of any relationship.

## The Brain and the Body
### Forward Thinking
Oldest parts of the brain are bottom and back, most basal.
The [[Frontal Lobe]] tends to be where reason and judgement are. Tackles uncertainty. It fully develops between the ages of 20 and 30.
Solving problems in school is unrelated to making good decisions under uncertainty, the difficulty of real life.
Even with damage to the frontal lobe, [[Neuroplasticity]]means that some of this can be regained.
Your 20s is the easiest time, with heightened neurplasticity, to become the person you want to become.
20s brain usage makes you a better 30 year old.

### Calm Yourself
Daily grind of work matters in a way schoolwork doesn't - a lot of work without obvious reward.
20s without anxiety about work generally is tied to overconfidence and underemployment
People vividly remember surprising events.
Traumatic events are lessons often.
The older you are, the more confidence that problems can be solved you have. 20somethings don't have that resilience.
Constant worrying leads to high [[Cortisol]].
Learning how to calm yourself down, and not outsource it to others, is a vital skill.

### Outside In
Mistakes are taken as a statement of who you are.
To gain confidence in a skill, you need repetition. You shouldn't be confident as a new employee.
[[Fixed Mindset]] would have you think that people innately have confidence in their jobs. A [[Growth Mindset]] would place you as being able to improve in it.
Real confidence comes from proof of mastery.
Easy successes don't make confidence, bouncing back from failures does.

### Getting Along and Getting Ahead
Research debates how much our personalities are fixed at 30. The optimistic side is "slightly."
20s are a time where you can seriously affect who you will become for better or worse while also laying the groundwork for career and marriage.
By changing your life as a 20 year old, you can change a lot about your mentality.
Making adult commitments tends to lead to happiness, staking an identity claim.
Staying single across your 20s almost always leads to more relationship dissatisfaction.

### Every Body
Management of fertility, for men and women, is important - much harder to have desired number of kids post 35.
Many women can't have kids, or need expensive fertility treatments, past 40.
1 in 20s, 1/2 at 30, 1/4 at 35, 1/8 at 40.
Average 40 year old couple spends 100k when the book was written to have one birth if they need fertility treatment, 42 means 300k.
IVF succeeds 10-20% of the time.
Delayed childbirth to 40s means taking care of retired parents and kids at the same time.

### Do the Math
The brain is very bad at keeping time without markers.
People are very biased to help themselves in the present and not the future.
Culturally we overvalue the 20s, the idea that after 40 there's nothing to life instead of the value of family. 20s need a reminder that we will keep living after.
Huge difference in having a life in your 30s and starting your life in your 30s.
You have to create an honest schedule of when you will fit in major life milestones.
Happy endings are intentional - begin with the last sentence.

## Epilogue - Will Things Work Out for Me?
Some things in life are just how they are.
Not knowing what the future holds doesn't mean that these years aren't real.
If you are paying attention to your life in your 20s, the real glory years will come.
There are no guarantees about the future - get to work, be intentional, decide your life.
