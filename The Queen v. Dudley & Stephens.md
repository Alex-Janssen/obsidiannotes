## Rule
Killing is murder even in necessity (escaping death by hunger).

## Holding
No necessity could justify the seamen in murdering the boy - they were guilty of murder.

## Posture
Jury allow court to decide - ignorant on matter.

## Facts
The prisoners were seamen who drifted away, and, on the twentieth day from land, they conspired and did kill a boy to eat his flesh as they had insufficient  food and water. Dudley and Stephens consented, Brooks dissented. After eating the boy's flesh, they were saved by a passing boat.

## Issue
Whether murder for necessary cannibalism is murder?

## Disposition
Guilty of murder. Sentenced to death.

## Reasoning
The men would have likely died of starvation without killing the boy, the boy earlier, and there was no sign of relief. Someone would have to be killed to save the others. The court finds Dudley and Stephens guilty of felony and murder. The idea that you can kill to save yourself without being threatened is spurious. You can kill an assailant to save yourself - not an innocent bystander. The necessity in this case, with being saved not clear even if the boy is killed, is not recognized in law. In a shipwreck, the captain has an obligation to the crew, and the crew to passengers; killing the child is inconceivable. The kid was killed as he was the weakest - no barometer of lives' worthiness can be held. 

## Learning Outcome
Absoluteness of homicide in English courts.

## Miscelaneous

and to lay down rules which we could not ourselves satisfy. But a man has no right to declare temptation to be an excuse, though he might himself have yielded to it, nor allow compassion for the criminal to change or weaken in any manner the legal definition of the crime"
