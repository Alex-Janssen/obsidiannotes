## Rule
Future payment is enforceable in the case of antecedent material benefit to the promisor, even if unrequested.

## Holding
McGowin's agreeing to pay Webb after being saved from bodily harm made the promise a valid contract.

## Posture
Appealed by Webb

## Facts
The appellant chose to fall with a large 75 lbs pine block he dropped in the course of normal employment to save the defendant from serious injury. The appellant was seriously handicapped for life, unable to find physical or mental labor afterward. McGowin agreed to pay $15 every two weeks until the end of the appellant's life and to care for him. These continued until McGowin's death on Jan 1, at which point they were discontinued on Jan 27 - Webb sued for recovery of the unpaid installments.

## Issue
Whether there was sufficient consideration to enforce the biweekly payments as originally agreed by the late McGowin.

## Disposition
Reversed and remanded

## Reasoning
McGowin received material benefit far greater than financial aid and became morally bound to compensate the appellant - the agreement to pay Webb is legally enforceable. Material benefit is received for caring/improving/preserving the property of a promisee even if initially unrequested, and thus future payment is enforceable. Boothe v. Fitzpatrick, 36 Vt. 681 saw a bull being tended by a plaintiff - a promise to pay after its tending was enforceable due to the subsequent promise being equivalent to a previous request. Saving their life would have been more material than caring for a bull. Life has more than sentimental value - it has material value (eg insurance). Moral obligation is sufficient consideration in the case of receipt of a material benefit. Subsequent promises to pay for a previously rendered service ratify the services originally rendered - and thus McGowin's promise to pay affirmed what the appellant had provided him. Benefit to promisor and injury to promisee are valid consideration, and the fact that McGowin did agree to pay means the actions of Webb were not gratiutous.

## Learning Outcome
Limits of [[Mills v. Wyman]] - antecedent benefit as enforceable in the case of emergency care and subsequent promise to pay.
